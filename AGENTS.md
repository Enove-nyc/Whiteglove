## Customer-facing copy

- **Provide information, not hashkafa.** Describe what a place is and what visiting involves; leave the paskening to the traveler and their rov. Do not tell a customer whether to go, do not write "ask your rov," and do not frame an attraction as a choice to make carefully. A church, a museum of religious art, a gallery with nudes — say plainly what it is and stop there. What the site *lists at all* is still a selection decision (no nightlife, bars, mixed concerts, casinos — see below); how it *describes* what it lists is information only. This is a standing general rule, not specific to any one place.
- Write public copy in a natural, confident and concise voice suitable for an established travel website.
- Keep the site-wide under-production banner visible until the owner explicitly asks to remove it. The banner may be polished, but not deleted or hidden.
- Do not expose internal workflows or content status to customers. Avoid phrases such as “unverified,” “being checked,” “on record,” “research queue,” “not published yet,” or explanations that the owner has not completed something.
- Hide unfinished or empty public sections until they contain useful customer-ready content.
- Do not make blanket payment promises such as “no card taken” or “nothing charged.” State a price or payment step only when it is accurate and needed.
- Keep vacation discovery, self-service planning and partner booking links as the primary public journey. Heritage places are part of the normal destination directory, not a separate section (see "Personal trip planning has been removed" below for when this changed and why).
- There is no personal trip-planning or booking-assistance offer anywhere on the site, discreet or otherwise — see "Personal trip planning has been removed" below.
- Audience-appropriate is not the same as kosher-only. Vacation attractions and lodging do not have to be Jewish places or kosher establishments; prefer Jewish when it fits, and keep ordinary sightseeing and stays that Orthodox / Torah-observant travelers would use. Never promote nightlife, clubs, mixed concerts, or similar venues. Reserve “kosher” for food / kashrus features — do not demand a kosher label on every attraction, hotel, cemetery, shul or mikvah. Do not blur this into the kosher food finder or the heritage section. Public wording for this standard lives in `data/listing-audience.ts` (footer, About, Where to stay) — reuse it; do not invent a second version. It was taken off Things to do at the owner's word; do not put it back there.

## Personal trip planning has been removed

**Done-for-you itinerary planning (`/services`) does not exist on this site.** It used to be kept as a reachable last resort — mentioned last, never promoted — and the owner then decided to remove it outright rather than keep it available: no page, no nav entry, no footer link, no form, no mention anywhere, not even discreetly through Contact.

What follows from that:

- Do not re-add `/services`, a services page, a "have us plan it" card, or any wording that offers personal planning or booking assistance, in any form or at any prominence. This is a full removal, not a demotion — do not partially restore it as "just a footer link" or "just reachable from Contact."
- **Never ask the owner to price it.** No starting price, no typical range, no turnaround time, no "does the fee come off a booking", no cancellation or refund terms, no post-itinerary support window. There is nothing on this site to price. This is a standing answer, not an open question — do not re-raise it as an outstanding item.
- The three free tools are the whole offer: get recommendations (`/plan`), build the trip yourself (`/itinerary`), search booking partners (`/book`).

## Settled decisions — do not re-open these

This section is the memory that carries across chats. It is loaded at the start of every session, so a decision recorded here is not re-litigated in a later one. When the owner settles something — a decision or a standing preference — write it here in plain terms, in his words where they are clear, so the next chat starts already knowing it and he does not have to say it twice.

- **Which repository a trip feature belongs in is decided BEFORE it is written, not after.** This has gone wrong repeatedly and always the same way: the work happens in whichever repository the session happened to open, and Itineraries — the product whose entire job is building, organising and managing a trip — ends up behind on its own features. Forwarding a confirmation in by email was built on Kosher Travel first. So were the packing list, itinerary optimisation, itinerary translation, offline documents and trip updates.

  THE TEST IS WHOSE JOB THE FEATURE IS, NOT WHICH FOLDER IS OPEN. Anything that BUILDS, ORGANISES or MANAGES a trip — flights, hotels, transport, documents, day-by-day, confirmations, reminders, what changed since it was planned — is Itineraries work and is built in the itineraries repository FIRST. Anything that helps somebody DISCOVER or PLAN — destinations, kosher food, shuls, mikvaos, heritage, maps, local services — is Kosher Travel work and is built here. A feature that genuinely serves both is still built in Itineraries first and then ported, because that is the direction the drift never goes on its own.

  AND THE PORT IS PART OF THE JOB, NOT A FOLLOW-UP. The two deployments run from two codebases, so merging here does not put anything on whitegloveitineraries.com. A trip feature is not finished until it exists on the side it is for. When only one side can be done in a sitting, say so in plain words in the same message — never leave it implied.

  THE ONE THING THAT STAYS ONE-DIRECTIONAL is the marketing link: `ContinueInItineraries` and `lib/itineraries-handoff.ts` are Kosher Travel's alone and must never be ported. Their absence from the itineraries repository is correct, not drift.

- **Two platforms, one White Glove.** White Glove is a travel technology company with two distinct but connected platforms.

  WHITE GLOVE KOSHER TRAVEL — this repo — is a free travel information and planning platform for kosher and Jewish travel. Its customer is the traveller, and its job is DISCOVER & PLAN: destinations, kosher food, shuls, mikvaos, places to stay, attractions, heritage, maps and local services, so a traveller can work out where to go and what to include.

  WHITE GLOVE ITINERARIES is a general travel itinerary and trip-management platform for travellers, advisers and agencies. Its job is BUILD, ORGANISE & MANAGE: complete digital itineraries with flights, hotels, transport, activities, documents, maps, schedules and contacts. **It is not a kosher travel product** and must not be described as one.

  THE CONNECTION IS ONE-DIRECTIONAL, and only in marketing. Kosher Travel may promote and connect a traveller to White Glove Itineraries when they want their planning turned into a full itinerary. White Glove Itineraries never promotes Kosher Travel and stays independently positioned as general travel — do not add anything on that side that points here.

  BEHIND THEM, ONE ACCOUNT AND ONE DATABASE. One White Glove account and login across both, with trip information and subscription status following the customer between them; an eligible paid subscription can unlock paid benefits on both as those are built. The travel database is shared, so an itinerary builder can search it and pull places straight into a trip. This is the intended architecture, not a merge: they remain two products with two front doors, today served by two separate deployments from two codebases (nothing built in this repo reaches whitegloveitineraries.com). Do not propose collapsing them into one product, and do not link this site to a path that only exists on the other one — an outbound link to that site's own domain is the connection, a cross-domain path is a 404. Which means an adviser feature — a group screen, a client's boarding pass, anything whose user is somebody planning for other people — has to be built or ported in the itineraries repository as well, and merging it here does not put it on that site. Both were built here first on 26 August and `/group` 404'd on whitegloveitineraries.com until they were ported; check which platform a feature is FOR before choosing where to build it.

  THE SHARED STORE IS CONFIRMED, not aspirational — tested in production on 1 September 2026. An account registered through whitegloveitineraries.com was immediately recognised by whiteglovekoshertravel.com (its login answered "still waiting for its verification code", which `verifyAccountStatus` only reaches for a record it actually found). Both deployments read the same Upstash Redis and the same `white-glove:*` key namespace, so accounts, trips, saved places, travel preferences and share tokens are genuinely one set of data. The two Railway projects are separate and neither contains a database service; each points at the same external store through its own variables. Postgres was not provable from outside — the guide paths are 410 on the itineraries domain, so no Postgres-backed page is public there — but it holds directory content rather than anything a traveller owns.

  TWO CONSEQUENCES WORTH KEEPING. First: a shared store is NOT single sign-on. Cookies are scoped to a domain, so a session on one site is not a session on the other — the same credentials work on both, which is a different promise and the one to make. Second, and the live hazard: both services run `npm run db:migrate` before every deploy against that shared Postgres. The two repos' schemas and all 17 migrations are identical today, so nothing conflicts. The day a feature adds a migration in one repo only, the two deploys race and one app runs against a schema it does not expect. Check the other repo's migrations before adding one.

- **Adviser features are built in the itineraries repository, not this one.** Kosher Travel is DISCOVER & PLAN; Itineraries is BUILD, ORGANISE & MANAGE. Anything whose user is somebody planning a trip *for other people* — clients, proposals, payments, extras, client forms, group trips, commissions, the pipeline, the trip context bar — belongs there. This was already written a few paragraphs up and it lost every time, because it was one sentence inside a long paragraph and nothing checked it: the entire advisor CRM was written here and served from whiteglovekoshertravel.com, and three of its screens did not exist on the itineraries side at all. `tests/advisor-tools-live-on-itineraries.test.ts` enforces it now — it holds a list of the adviser screens still served here that may only ever get shorter, and it fails the build if a new one appears. The screens were ported to the itineraries repository on 31 August; taking them off this deployment is the remaining step. **Before building anything for an adviser, ask which platform it is FOR, and build it there.**

- **The gear shelf is not to be spread across the site.** It has two homes — `/travel-gear`, and the packing list, where a traveller is already reading a list of things they have to go and buy. That is the whole of it. The owner turned down putting gear links on destination pages and through the planner to chase the three Amazon qualifying sales, and his reason is the one to remember: a site that pushes gear everywhere ends up looking like a travel-gear website, and the guide is what has the value. Do not propose it again, and do not add gear links to a new surface without him asking.

- **The site notice stays exactly as it is**, a full-screen popup, until the owner says otherwise. He was shown the cost (it blocks every automated functional check and is the whole of the tab-order findings) and chose to keep it. Do not raise it again, and do not quietly turn it into a strip.
- **The About page carries no personal facts at all** — no name, no background, no photograph, no years of experience, and no location. White Glove is not based anywhere: it is a website. Do not ask him for any of them, and do not treat the empty fields on `/admin/settings/about` as gaps. The page is finished as it stands: what the site is for, and what its information is worth.
- **Vacation attractions need not be Jewish or kosher.** Audience-appropriate ≠ kosher-only. A Jewish venue is better when available, but general sightseeing, parks, museums, family activities and ordinary lodging are fine when Orthodox / Torah-observant travelers would go. No mixed concerts, clubs, nightlife, bars, casinos, or similar. Do not require a kosher label on attractions, lodging, cemeteries, shuls or mikvaos — “kosher” is for food / kashrus features. Kosher food tools stay kosher-specific. Customer-facing copy is in `data/listing-audience.ts` and must stay consistent with this.
- **Heritage is not a separate top-level section any more.** Heritage destinations (towns, kevarim, cemeteries) are part of the normal destination directory, reached the same way any other destination is; practical heritage information (kevarim, cemeteries, shuls) is reached through Kosher. The heritage pages themselves still exist and stay reachable — only the standalone "Heritage" category is gone. Do not reintroduce Heritage as its own top-level nav item or reopen the old "vacation vs. heritage" split without the owner asking for it.
- **Ratings ask about White Glove, not the trip.** White Glove does not arrange anyone's trip, so it never asks a traveler how the trip itself turned out. The trip rating asks only how White Glove did during the trip — how the site and its information held up. A listing rating stays about the place. Do not reword these to report on the trip's outcome.
- **Sources live on one `/sources` page, never on the listings.** A listing shows no source line at all — not even small print — because a source shown beside a listing, a kever especially, reads as an endorsement of whatever it points at. Every source the site cites is credited once on `/sources`, grouped and framed as acknowledgement rather than endorsement, and linked quietly from the footer. That page is generated from the data by `scripts/build-sources-index.mjs` into `data/sources-index.generated.ts` — rebuild it after source data changes, do not hand-edit it. The one exception is the heritage-cemetery page (`/cemeteries/heritage/[slug]`), which holds no details of its own and keeps its normal "See the details on Nesiya Tova" button. Do not put source links back onto individual listings.
- **Pricing is Personal / Trip Pass / Advisor Starter / Advisor Pro; Agency is talk-to-us.** PERSONAL is free and is a real plan, not an empty account: unlimited trips in the itinerary planner, unlimited printing, and the whole kosher guide. The planner is the free product and must not be crippled to sell the tier above it. TRIP PASS ($9, one-time) adds the White Glove app on the traveller's own phone through the trip — it does NOT cap trips (it was "One Trip", capped at one trip ever, at $15; a cap on the paid plan would have meant paying to keep fewer trips than free). ADVISOR STARTER ($29/mo) and ADVISOR PRO ($49/mo) are subscriptions for people planning for other people. AGENCY (several advisers under one subscription, genuine multi-login) is a "talk to us" door on the pricing page linking `/contact?reason=agency` — no price, no card, no coming-soon badge; do not build seats without the owner asking. ENTERPRISE likewise stays talk-to-us. The plan KEYS in `lib/account-plans.ts` (`free`, `one_trip`, `starter`, `pro`) are permanent — they are stored on live accounts and sent to Stripe — so renaming a plan changes `PLAN_LABELS`, never the key. AMOUNTS ARE NEVER WRITTEN IN CODE: they are set in Stripe / `/admin/settings/plans` and printed only by `offerLine()`; `tests/plan-comparison.test.ts` fails if a `$` and a digit appear on the pricing page or in the comparison data. See `lib/account-plans.ts` and `lib/account-limits.ts` for exactly what each plan includes.
- **The White Glove app opens ONE TRIP at a time unless the plan says otherwise.** A Trip Pass is a token the account holds and SPENDS on a single trip (`lib/trip-pass.ts`, `lib/trip-pass-store.ts`) — a second trip is a second pass. It is not a plan grant: buying it used to set the account to `one_trip` and open every trip that account would ever have, which is a subscription sold as a single fee. Advisor Starter and Advisor Pro carry the app on every trip they run (`appOnEveryTrip`). The one gate is `mayOpenTripInApp` in `lib/companion-access.ts` — plan first, then the trip's pass; no door should ask either question itself. `companionApp` still means "the app is part of this plan", which is what the pricing page advertises. Handing a trip to a *client* stays Advisor Starter and up, behind the separate `companionClients` gate: naming a client, sending it, and creating a client code. Advisor Pro adds its own name and logo on the client app in place of White Glove's (`ownBranding`) — a further gate on top of `companionClients`, not a replacement for it. A pass whose trip is deleted is released, which is not a way to move one.
- **A code carries messages only when somebody else is on the other end.** Every per-trip code has a kind (`ShareKind` in `lib/account-store.ts`): a **client** code is an adviser handing a trip to the person taking it, and it carries the conversation; a **self** code is somebody carrying their own trip on their own phone, and it carries none. This is a fact about the CODE, not about the plan — an Advisor Pro opening her own family's trip gets the same silence as a Trip Pass holder, because in both cases the thread would be addressed to the reader. The block is two things and no more: `resolveCompanionShare` returns null for a self code, which closes the chat and report routes and anything added later in one place; and the page hands `CompanionApp` no `chat` prop, which removes the tab, the badge, the polling and the home-screen card together. Do not add a plan check for this, and do not hide the tab with CSS. A stored code with no kind reads as "client" — every code that existed before kinds did was made by an adviser to send to somebody.
- **The kosher homepage is settled: search-only hero, Featured is the six sections.** The opening is the search and nothing above it — no headline, no eyebrow, no pitch paragraph, no browse links; the `<h1>` stays `sr-only` so the page has one for search engines without showing it. Featured is the six main SECTIONS of the site (things to do, where to stay, kosher food, heritage, map, directory), not destinations ranked by what people opened — it was destinations once and the owner asked for the shape of the site instead. An outside audit has since recommended reversing both (a "what are you trying to do?" panel above the search, and a "Popular now" list of destinations); he was asked directly and said both stand as he set them. Do not re-raise either, and do not implement them from a fresh audit. `tests/homepage-sections.test.ts` pins both. The separate "Explore" list that used to sit below Featured is gone for a different reason: six of its eight links were an exact copy of the Featured cards, left over from when Featured showed destinations. About and Verification, its only non-duplicates, are in the footer.

- **The packing list (`/packing`) is open to everyone.** It used to be signed-in only, so the one page whose whole subject is a list of things to go and buy sent a visitor to the login door. Every visitor now gets a starter list (`data/packing-basics.ts`) they can check off in the browser; somebody with a trip in the planner gets the list generated from that trip in its place, saved to their account as before. It is linked from the footer and from Travel gear, and is indexable. Do not put the sign-in gate back.

- **One admin dashboard runs both sides.** The owner is the only person who signs into it, so it is not split per brand and must not be: everything for Kosher Travel and everything for Itineraries lives on the same dashboard, reached the same way. Its own chrome is therefore brand-neutral — "White Glove admin", never one company's name — while each screen inside stays whatever it is (shuls and hechsherim are kosher work; plans and client apps are itineraries work; both belong here). Do not build a second dashboard, a per-brand admin, or a brand switcher for it, and do not add an admin screen to only one of the two deployments.

- **Four tiers, and the free account is a benefit rather than a gate.** NO ACCOUNT explores and searches Kosher Travel freely — browsing and searching the public site must never require signing in. A FREE KOSHER TRAVEL ACCOUNT adds save, remember, personalise and continue: saved places, saved trips, resuming where you left off across devices, reusable travel preferences, an assistant that can use those preferences and your earlier planning, recommendations that improve from them, and updates on saved trips where real data exists. WHITE GLOVE ITINERARIES builds and organises the actual trip. A TRIP PASS upgrades one trip to the app on the phone.

  ASK FOR THE ACCOUNT ONLY WHERE THERE IS A REASON — at the moment somebody saves a place, saves a trip, or would benefit from being remembered or continuing on another device. Never on arrival, never as a wall in front of browsing.

  MEMORY MUST NOT FEEL HIDDEN. Reusable travel preferences are visible in one place the traveller can read, edit and delete, kept separate from trip-specific detail, and not every sentence said to the assistant becomes a permanent preference. Do not advertise any of this publicly until it actually works.

- **The account remembers two things, and they are kept apart on purpose.** TRAVEL PREFERENCES (`data/travel-preferences.ts`) are what the traveller TOLD us — pace, interests, kashrus, Shabbos, lodging, transport, access. Durable, never trip-specific: no destination, dates or party size may live there, or the store starts answering next winter's question with last summer's trip. Every value comes from a fixed list (the planner's own vocabularies in `lib/trip-plan.ts`), which is a SECURITY property and not tidiness — these go into an assistant's prompt, so free text here would be a way to write instructions for a model. RECENT PLACES (`data/recent-places.ts`) are only WHERE THEY HAVE BEEN: eight entries, a fortnight, paths on this site only.

  NEITHER FEEDS THE OTHER, EVER. Looking at Rome twice is not a preference for Italy. Recent places are never read into preferences and never sent to a model; nothing is ever inferred into preferences from what somebody searched, opened or asked. Both have their own Forget button that really deletes, both are visible on `/account`, and `describeForAssistant()` is the ONLY path from either to a model — the traveller reads its exact output on that same screen, so a second path would make that line a lie. Tests hold all of this.

  Saving is ACCOUNT-FIRST for anything new (`components/useSavedPlaces.tsx`), not localStorage-then-sync like the older buttons — carrying a place between devices is why somebody made an account. And the account is asked for only at the moment it is worth something, in the words of the thing being saved ("Sign in to save this restaurant"), through the existing `useRequireSignIn`. Never a wall in front of browsing.

- **A client reaches a trip by a per-trip code, not a login.** Each trip has a unique code — its **share token** (a random, unguessable per-trip id from `ensureTripShare`), never the raw internal trip id, so a client cannot guess a nearby one and land on someone else's trip. An Advisor Starter or Advisor Pro adviser creates the code and sends it to that one client; it opens that one trip as the app and no other. The `/app` front page, for a visitor not already in their own app, offers two doors: **"Enter a code from your travel adviser"** (a client, no account — opens their one trip) and **"Log in"** (advisers and other paid members, who then see their own trips). Do not turn client access back into an account requirement, and do not use the raw trip id as the code.

## Working with the owner

**"Step by step" means one step, then stop.** Give a single step, wait for him to say done, and only then give the next one. Do not send a numbered list of five and call it step by step — he is working through these in his own dashboards, and the next step is useless until the one before it is done.

Say what the step is, where to do it, and how he will know it worked. Nothing else.
