Commissioned review of whitegloveitineraries.com, whiteglovekoshertravel.com and
both repositories. Recorded here verbatim below the line, unedited.

## How to read it

**This is an outside view, not a work order.** It is specific and largely
credible — its first finding is the same brand leakage the owner reported the
same day — but several claims are worth checking against the code before acting
on them, and at least one recommendation cuts against a decision already settled
in `AGENTS.md`. Check first, then do.

Already confirmed true at the time of filing:

- **The Navbar decides the brand after hydration.** `components/Navbar.tsx` sets
  the brand in a `useEffect`, which is also why it is one of the files carrying
  a standing `react-hooks/set-state-in-effect` violation. A visitor on the
  itineraries domain can be served kosher branding and see it change.
- **The two repositories are overlapping copies.** `Enove-nyc/Whiteglove-Itineraries`
  is a fork serving the itineraries domain from its own Railway service.
- **Shared pages describe the wrong service.** `/about` and `/contact` render
  wholly as White Glove Kosher Travel on the itineraries domain — not by
  redirecting, but because their copy in `data/pages.ts` is kosher-specific.
- **Email came from one address for both brands** — fixed on 25 August; the
  itineraries brand now sends from `RESEND_FROM_EMAIL_ITINERARIES`.

Worth checking before acting:

- The claim that the kosher homepage's `<h1>` is screen-reader-only, and that a
  second "Explore" section contradicts a comment saying it was removed.
- Image alternative text on both homepages.

**Where it conflicts with a settled decision, the settled decision wins** until
the owner says otherwise — see "Settled decisions" in `AGENTS.md`. The audit
recommends a pricing page and trial terms for Itineraries; pricing exists and is
settled (One Trip / Advisor Starter / Advisor Pro), and `/pricing` 404s on the
itineraries domain, which is a routing gap rather than a product question.

# White Glove full-site audit

Audited 25 August 2026: whitegloveitineraries.com, whiteglovekoshertravel.com, and the connected GitHub repositories `Enove-nyc/Whiteglove` and `Enove-nyc/Whiteglove-Itineraries`.

## Executive finding

The two properties have a sound technical base and a consistent visual system. Their main weakness is product definition. They look like two labels applied to one large application, although the audiences and buying decisions differ. White Glove Itineraries should sell a professional itinerary product to travel advisors. White Glove Kosher Travel should help an observant traveler judge whether a destination is workable. At present, shared navigation and shared routes blur those jobs.

The sites are usable, but the first visit does not establish enough trust or demonstrate enough value. The kosher homepage removes its visible headline and explanation. The itinerary homepage explains the idea but shows no product interface, sample itinerary, price, trial, proof, or clear advisor workflow. Competitors answer these questions immediately.

## Highest-priority findings

### P0 — Separate the product architecture

Both domains run substantially the same Next.js application. The itinerary homepage switches by host, but most other pages use a shared navbar whose brand is corrected in the browser after the page loads. The code explicitly starts with the kosher brand and changes it after mount on the itinerary domain. This may produce a brief incorrect brand, inconsistent server-rendered markup, and leakage of kosher-site concepts into the advisor product.

Use one codebase if desired, but define strict brand route maps. Each domain should have its own server-resolved layout, metadata, sitemap, navigation, footer, search scope, analytics property, structured data, and 404 behavior. Advisor routes can remain shared internally. Public kosher-directory routes should redirect to the kosher domain when opened on the itinerary domain. Product routes should redirect in the opposite direction where appropriate.

The two repositories also appear to contain overlapping copies of the same application. This increases deployment drift and doubles maintenance. Establish one canonical repository or a monorepo with shared packages. Archive the obsolete copy after confirming deployment ownership.

### P0 — Give each homepage a single, visible promise and action

The kosher homepage contains an H1, but it is screen-reader-only. A sighted visitor sees a search field without a clear statement of what the database includes, how records are checked, or why this search is better than Google. Show a concise heading and a short trust line above the search. Example: “Plan a kosher trip with the details that decide whether it works.” Follow it with “Restaurants, minyanim, mikvahs, Shabbos logistics, hotels and heritage sites, checked and dated.”

The itinerary homepage has a better headline, but “Build a trip” opens the planner before the visitor sees the product or understands the commercial terms. Replace the two equal actions with one main action, such as “Create a sample itinerary,” and a quieter “Watch a 90-second tour.” Add a real interface preview in the first screen.

### P0 — Demonstrate trust and freshness on the kosher site

Kosher and Shabbos information becomes unsafe when stale. Every listing should display:

- last checked date and source;
- status: verified, community-reported, or unconfirmed;
- certifying authority, with a direct source link where applicable;
- a visible “report an update” control;
- Shabbos-specific attributes, such as keys, elevators, walking distance, eruv, Friday arrival limits, and nearby minyan times.

The site has Verification and Sources pages, but trust information belongs beside each decision. A global disclaimer cannot replace record-level provenance.

### P1 — Remove competing and duplicated navigation

Desktop pages combine category menus, utility icons, a hamburger, and account entry. Mobile pages add a fixed four-item bottom bar plus the floating assistant. The result is repeated access to Search, Route, Itinerary, Sign in, and menu functions. The floating assistant also competes with the bottom-right navigation area.

On desktop, show the hamburger only below the desktop-menu breakpoint. On mobile, keep three primary tabs plus Menu. Put account access inside Menu unless frequent signed-in use proves it needs a permanent tab. Move the assistant into Search or provide one labeled “Ask” tab. Avoid two floating controls near the same thumb zone.

### P1 — Replace generic imagery with destination and product evidence

The live homepages expose only one logo image in the document; several large visual cards use the same default background. Repeated generic imagery weakens both discovery and perceived depth. The kosher homepage should show real destination images with accurate captions. The itinerary site should show its planner, client app, offline wallet, chat, and day view. Product screenshots will carry more value than decorative travel photography.

### P1 — Fix image accessibility

The logo image on both homepages has no useful alternative text. Either give the linked logo a clear accessible name and mark the image decorative, or supply suitable alt text. Audit content images in destination and listing pages as well. Do not derive alt text automatically from filenames.

### P1 — Clarify conversion and pricing for Itineraries

Mature itinerary products disclose the workflow, trial, pricing direction, integrations, app behavior, and data handling. White Glove currently asks visitors to enter the tool without answering those questions. Add:

- a product tour with advisor and client views;
- a transparent pricing page and trial terms;
- a sample client itinerary that requires no sign-in;
- an integrations and import section;
- security, privacy, support, and ownership information;
- advisor proof: named case studies, metrics, and screenshots.

### P1 — Improve first-use flows

For Kosher Travel, begin with a destination or constraint. A useful guided planner can ask where the traveler is considering, dates, kashrut standard, Shabbos needs, party type, and transport. Return feasible areas, warnings, and a saved comparison. Search alone assumes the visitor already knows the destination.

For Itineraries, offer an import-first path. Advisors should be able to paste confirmation emails, upload supplier PDFs, duplicate a previous trip, or start from a template. A blank day-by-day editor is slower than the products it competes with.

## Cross-site clashes

| Area | Current clash | Recommended rule |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Traveler directory and advisor software share one application identity | Kosher domain speaks to travelers; itinerary domain speaks to advisors and clients |
| Navigation | Route and Itinerary appear on both properties without clear context | Explain these as personal tools on Kosher; present them as professional workflow on Itineraries |
| Assistant | The same floating Ask control appears across both sites | Give each assistant a narrow scope and domain-specific label |
| Brand | Shared layout can initially render the kosher brand on itinerary pages | Resolve brand on the server from the host |
| Search | Itinerary domain has little searchable public content | Search trips/templates on Itineraries; search places and services on Kosher |
| Footer/legal | Shared pages risk describing the wrong service | Maintain brand-specific About, pricing, terms, privacy summaries, and support paths |

## Device review

### Mobile

The bottom navigation uses suitable large targets and includes safe-area positioning. The interface becomes crowded because the bottom bar, assistant, sticky header, and hamburger all remain active. Keep persistent controls to one navigation surface. Confirm that every page reserves enough bottom padding so the last button or form field is not covered. Test 320, 360, 390, and 430 pixel widths, browser text at 200%, landscape mode, and installed-PWA mode.

The brand wordmark is hidden below 360 pixels. Retain an accessible home label and consider a compact text mark so the smallest supported device still names the service.

### Tablet

Tablet receives the mobile-style menu because desktop categories begin around 1024 pixels. This is reasonable, but wide tablets have enough room for a shorter primary navigation. Test iPad portrait and landscape separately. Cards should not become large empty panels merely because a three-column desktop grid collapses.

### Desktop

The sticky header is clear, but category dropdowns plus utility icons and the hamburger create excess choice. The hamburger should not appear at full desktop widths. Dropdown behavior is technically elaborate; simpler click-to-open behavior will be more predictable than mixing hover state with press state.

## Design-system review

The navy, cream, muted gold, display serif, and Inter body type create a restrained premium identity. Contrast appears generally strong. The weak point is repetition: cream cards, gold borders, rounded rectangles, and navy gradients are used for most levels of importance. Introduce clearer hierarchy through spacing, scale, imagery, and fewer bordered containers. Reserve gold for selected states, trust marks, and primary emphasis.

Standardize button language. The live pages use “Plan,” “Book,” “The app,” “Build a trip,” “See the app,” “SEARCH,” “AI ASSISTANT,” and “ASK.” Use verbs that state the result: “Find a destination,” “Build an itinerary,” “View sample,” and “Ask about kosher travel.” Sentence case will feel calmer than mixed all-caps controls.

## Accessibility and usability checks

Positive findings include a skip link in the shared layout, semantic headings, keyboard-aware menus, minimum 44-pixel control targets, and labeled search input. No broken homepage images or console errors appeared during the audit.

Further work:

- run automated checks across every public route, then complete keyboard and screen-reader testing;
- test focus containment and focus return in menus, sign-in dialogs, search overlays, and the assistant;
- make errors specific and connect them to their fields;
- verify reduced-motion behavior and visible focus at every interactive element;
- confirm status does not rely on gold, red, or green alone;
- add captions or transcripts to product demonstrations;
- test large text without clipped sticky or fixed navigation.

## Competitive position

Travefy leads with a visible product, free-trial action, drag-and-drop workflow, reusable content, supplier integrations, mobile apps, offline access, live flight updates, CRM, proposals, and public proof. White Glove can compete through a calmer interface, stronger client handoff, and a narrower advisor workflow, but the site must demonstrate those advantages before asking for sign-in.

Kosher-directory competitors compete mainly on inventory breadth, destination connectivity, filters, freshness, and source authority. White Glove can distinguish itself through decision-quality information: where to stay for Shabbos, which claims were checked, what changed, and how the pieces of a trip fit together. This is more defensible than copying a large directory count.

## Repository findings

- Both connected repositories appear to contain overlapping versions of the same large Next.js application. Confirm which repository deploys each domain.
- Host-based branding is partly decided in a server page and partly corrected after hydration in the Navbar. Move it to a server-resolved brand provider.
- The public homepage component contains extensive historical comments. Move decision history to architecture records; keep source comments close to current behavior.
- Homepage device targeting is based on a user-agent regular expression for promotions. Prefer responsive delivery or client hints because tablets and new user agents will be misclassified.
- The shared layout loads several global client features on every page: tracking, service worker registration, account state, booking context, sign-in context, assistant, and a partner script. Load optional features only where required and measure their cost.
- The kosher homepage intentionally uses a visually hidden H1. Restore a visible heading.
- The source contains a second “Explore” section even though comments state that it was removed as duplication. Reconcile comments, product intent, and rendered content.
- Security headers and an enforced CSP are present in the main repository. This is a good base. Verify the same configuration is deployed on both domains and that reports are monitored.

## Recommended delivery order

### First two weeks

Confirm repository and deployment ownership. Add domain-specific route maps and server-side branding. Restore the kosher homepage promise. Add a product screenshot and sample itinerary to the itinerary homepage. Remove duplicated mobile controls. Fix image alternatives and run a route-wide accessibility scan.

### Following month

Create record-level verification design, destination comparison, a sample itinerary, pricing, and the advisor product tour. Add analytics funnels for search-to-listing, listing-to-save, sample-to-sign-up, and itinerary-created-to-shared.

### Following quarter

Build import, reusable content, offline destination packs, Shabbos planning, collaboration, and integrations. Measure adoption before adding more top-level navigation.

## Success measures

Track successful destination searches, searches with no useful result, listing saves, report-update submissions, itinerary completion, client-link opens, advisor activation, trial conversion, and return use. Measure Core Web Vitals by domain and device. Pair these with short task tests: “Can a traveler decide where to stay for Shabbos in Rome?” and “Can an advisor publish a three-day client itinerary from existing confirmations?”
